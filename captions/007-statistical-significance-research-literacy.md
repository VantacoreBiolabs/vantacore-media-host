“STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT” DOES NOT MEAN “IMPORTANT” 📊

A p-value can help show how compatible observed data are with a statistical model. It does not measure the size or importance of an effect, prove a hypothesis is true, or guarantee that another study will get the same result.

Before accepting a research headline, ask:

1️⃣ What is the effect size? A tiny difference can still reach statistical significance.

2️⃣ How wide is the confidence interval? A wide interval may signal substantial uncertainty about the estimate.

3️⃣ Has the finding been replicated? One positive result is a starting point—not the end of the evaluation.

Strong research literacy means reading past “p < 0.05” and looking at context, magnitude, uncertainty, study design and replication.
