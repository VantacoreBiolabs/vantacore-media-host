PEPTIDE, PROTEIN, OR SMALL MOLECULE? 🧬

These labels describe what a compound is built from—not whether it is automatically better, safer or more effective.

PEPTIDE
A relatively short chain of amino acids linked by peptide bonds. For one FDA regulatory distinction, chains of 40 or fewer amino acids are treated as peptides rather than proteins.

PROTEIN
A longer, more structurally complex amino-acid chain. Its three-dimensional folding can be essential to how it functions.

SMALL MOLECULE
A compact chemical compound rather than an amino-acid chain. SLU-PP-332, for example, belongs in this category—not the peptide category.

Important nuance: the peptide-versus-protein boundary can vary by scientific or regulatory context. Classification alone does not establish purity, approval status, safety, effectiveness or the strength of the human evidence.
