SURROGATE ENDPOINT vs CLINICAL OUTCOME 🔬

A study reports that a lab value improved. Does that automatically prove people felt better, functioned better or lived longer?

Not necessarily.

A SURROGATE ENDPOINT is a substitute marker expected to predict a clinical benefit. Examples can include a laboratory measurement, scan, biomarker or physical sign.

A CLINICAL OUTCOME directly measures something meaningful to people—how they feel, function or survive.

Surrogate endpoints can make research faster and more practical, especially when direct outcomes take years to measure. But the connection between the marker and the real-world benefit must be supported for that exact disease, intervention and context.

When reading a study, ask:

1️⃣ Was the surrogate endpoint validated for this context?
2️⃣ How large was the change?
3️⃣ Did the study also report direct clinical outcomes?
4️⃣ Was follow-up long enough?
5️⃣ What happened with safety?

A marker changing is evidence—but it is not automatically proof of meaningful benefit.
