SEMAX vs SELANK: SAME CATEGORY, DIFFERENT RESEARCH 🧠

These synthetic heptapeptides are often grouped together online, but they have different structures, origins and research questions.

SEMAX
• Built from the ACTH(4–7) sequence with a Pro-Gly-Pro extension.
• Preclinical studies have examined neurotrophin signaling, including BDNF-related pathways.
• Animal and cell research has also reported changes in gene expression connected to neural plasticity and inflammatory signaling.

SELANK
• A tuftsin-derived seven-amino-acid analog.
• Laboratory and animal studies have examined GABA-related neurotransmission and changes in gene expression.
• Other preclinical work has explored immune-signaling pathways.

WHAT ABOUT COMBINING THEM?
Mechanistic differences do not prove that combining two compounds creates an added benefit. Interaction studies, controlled human comparisons and long-term safety data would be needed. “Different pathways” is not the same as “proven synergy.”

Important: much of the mechanistic literature is preclinical, and the available human evidence is limited. U.S. safety and effectiveness have not been established. FDA has also identified safety concerns relevant to compounded Selank and is evaluating Semax-related bulk substances.

This comparison is educational—not a stacking recommendation or protocol.
