PEPTIDE OF THE DAY: THYMOSIN ALPHA-1 🧬

Thymosin alpha-1 (Tα1) is a naturally occurring, N-terminally acetylated 28-amino-acid peptide first isolated from thymic tissue. Its synthetic form is also called thymalfasin.

Instead of acting as a simple “immune booster,” Tα1 is studied as an immune-signaling modulator. Laboratory research has examined its interactions with dendritic cells, T-cell responses, Toll-like-receptor pathways, cytokine signaling and immune tolerance.

Clinical research has explored Tα1 across several immune-related conditions, but the evidence varies by indication, study quality and regulatory setting. Results from one disease—or from a biomarker change—should not be generalized to everyone.

Tα1 is not FDA-approved in the United States. FDA has also identified potential immunogenicity and impurity-related risks for compounded products. This post is educational, not medical guidance or a protocol.
