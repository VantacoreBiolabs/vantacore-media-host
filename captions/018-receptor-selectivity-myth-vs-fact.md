MYTH VS FACT: RECEPTOR SELECTIVITY 🎯

MYTH: A “selective” compound interacts with only one biological target.

FACT: Selectivity usually describes preference, not absolute exclusivity. A compound may bind or activate one target more strongly than others while still interacting with additional targets under some conditions.

Three details matter:

AFFINITY — How strongly a compound binds to a target.

EXPOSURE — As concentration changes, interactions that were minor in one experiment may become more relevant.

CONTEXT — Receptor abundance, tissue type, timing, metabolism and the model being studied can all influence the observed effect.

That is why a selective mechanism does not automatically mean a compound is side-effect-free, clinically effective or safe. Researchers still examine secondary pharmacology, exposure and real-world outcomes.

Save this the next time you see “highly selective” in a study.
