AGONIST vs PARTIAL AGONIST vs ANTAGONIST 🧬

These terms describe what happens after a compound binds to a receptor—and binding alone does not tell the whole story.

AGONIST
Binds and activates a receptor, producing a biological response in the system being measured.

PARTIAL AGONIST
Also binds and activates the receptor, but produces a lower maximum response than a full agonist in that same experimental system—even when receptor occupancy is high.

ANTAGONIST
Binds without activating the receptor. By occupying the receptor, it can block or reduce access by an agonist.

Important nuance: these labels are context-dependent. Receptor abundance, coupling efficiency, tissue type and the measured signaling pathway can influence the observed response. A compound may appear fully active in one system and partially active in another.

“Partial” does not automatically mean weak, ineffective or safer. It describes efficacy at a receptor under defined conditions—not the compound’s overall clinical value or risk.
