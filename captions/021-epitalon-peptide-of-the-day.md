PEPTIDE OF THE DAY: EPITALON 🧬

Epitalon—also spelled Epithalon—is a synthetic tetrapeptide built from four amino acids: alanine, glutamic acid, aspartic acid and glycine (AEDG).

Why is it studied?

CELL RESEARCH
Laboratory studies have examined whether Epitalon influences telomerase-related pathways and telomere biology in cultured human cells.

PRECLINICAL RESEARCH
Animal and cell models have explored circadian signaling, pineal biology, oxidative processes and aging-related mechanisms. Results have not been uniform; one mouse study reported no increase in mean lifespan.

EVIDENCE LIMIT
Cellular findings do not prove that a compound slows human aging or extends human life. Published human evidence for Epitalon remains limited, and FDA’s 2026 review found no clinical data establishing the safety of Epitalon free base or acetate for the evaluated compounded use.

Epitalon is not FDA-approved for human use. Longevity claims should be treated as hypotheses—not established clinical outcomes.
