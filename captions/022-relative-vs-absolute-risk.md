RELATIVE RISK vs ABSOLUTE RISK 📊

A headline says “50% lower risk.” That may sound dramatic—but without the starting risks, the number is incomplete.

EXAMPLE
If an outcome changes from 2 in 100 people to 1 in 100:

• Relative change: 50% lower
• Absolute change: 1 percentage point lower

Both statements describe the same result. Relative risk compares the two rates as a ratio. Absolute risk shows the actual difference between them.

When reading research, ask:
• What were the starting risks?
• How many people experienced the outcome?
• How long was the follow-up?
• How wide were the confidence intervals?

Large relative changes can come from small absolute differences. Strong evidence communication reports both.
