MYTH vs FACT: MORE TESTS, MORE FALSE-POSITIVE RISK 📊

MYTH: Every result with p < 0.05 deserves the same confidence.

FACT: When many hypotheses, outcomes, subgroups or time points are tested, the chance of finding at least one apparently significant result by chance can increase unless multiplicity is addressed.

That does not mean every significant finding is false. It means the analysis needs context.

Before accepting the headline, ask:

• How many statistical tests were performed?
• Were the primary outcomes specified before the data were examined?
• Were secondary or exploratory findings clearly labeled?
• Did the analysis use an appropriate method to control false-positive risk?
• Has the finding been independently replicated?

FDA guidance treats multiplicity as an important trial-design issue because uncontrolled testing can produce false or misleading conclusions.

One p-value is not the entire evidence story.
