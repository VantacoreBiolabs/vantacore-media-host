POTENCY vs EFFICACY 📈

These terms describe different features of a concentration-response relationship—and they are not interchangeable.

POTENCY
How much of a compound is needed to produce a defined response in a particular experimental system? Potency is often compared using EC50: the concentration that produces 50% of that compound’s maximum observed response. A lower EC50 generally indicates greater potency under those conditions.

EFFICACY
What is the greatest response observed in that system? Efficacy is often compared using Emax, the plateau of the concentration-response curve.

Two compounds can reach the same Emax at different concentrations. They would differ in potency but show similar maximal observed efficacy. Another pair can have similar EC50 values yet reach different maximum responses.

Important context: EC50 and Emax can shift with receptor abundance, tissue, signaling pathway, assay design and other experimental conditions. Potency is not the same as affinity, and greater potency does not automatically mean greater clinical effectiveness, better safety or higher quality.

Always ask: potency and efficacy for which outcome, in which system?
