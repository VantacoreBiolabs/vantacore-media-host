CAGRILINTIDE: NOT A GLP-1 🧬

Cagrilintide belongs to a different hormone pathway than semaglutide or tirzepatide.

Four quick facts:

• It is a long-acting analogue of amylin, a peptide hormone normally released by the pancreas.  
• It acts through amylin-receptor pathways—not the GLP-1 receptor.  
• Amylin biology includes signals involved in satiety, meal-related glucagon regulation and gastric emptying.  
• Researchers have studied cagrilintide alone and combined with semaglutide, a combination called CagriSema.

Evidence check: published randomized trials have reported research signals, but cagrilintide remains under active clinical study. Trial findings do not guarantee approval, individual benefit or long-term safety.
