KISSPEPTIN-10: A SIGNAL UPSTREAM OF GnRH 🧬

Kisspeptin-10 is a short peptide with an important place in reproductive-hormone research.

Four quick facts:

• It is a 10-amino-acid member of the kisspeptin family, which is encoded by the KISS1 gene.  
• It binds a receptor called KISS1R, also known as GPR54.  
• Kisspeptin signaling sits upstream of GnRH, which then helps regulate the pituitary hormones LH and FSH.  
• Human studies have measured hormone responses to Kisspeptin-10, while other work has examined related kisspeptin forms and analogues.

Evidence check: these studies help explain the biology, but they do not make Kisspeptin-10 an established treatment. Human studies have often been small or short, and long-term safety and clinical effectiveness for proposed uses remain open research questions.
