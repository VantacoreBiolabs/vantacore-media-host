BIOAVAILABILITY vs BIOACTIVITY: NOT THE SAME THING 🎯

These two terms answer different research questions.

Four quick facts:

• Bioavailability asks how quickly and how much active material becomes available at its site of action.  
• Bioactivity asks whether a substance produces a measurable biological response in a particular test system.  
• A compound can look highly active in a laboratory assay yet have limited availability in the body.  
• Formulation, absorption and breakdown can affect availability, while the target, cells and assay conditions can affect measured activity.

Evidence check: both terms are context-specific. Neither a bioavailability result nor a bioactivity result alone proves clinical benefit, safety or real-world effectiveness.
