PLACEBO CONTROL vs ACTIVE CONTROL 🔬

Both create a comparison group—but they answer different questions.

Four quick facts:

• A placebo control uses an inactive look-alike when that design is appropriate.  
• It helps separate the treatment effect from expectations, natural change and other study influences.  
• An active control compares the new intervention with an established treatment.  
• Active-control trials can ask whether the new option is better, similar or not unacceptably worse—but the exact claim depends on the study design.

Evidence check: neither control is automatically “better.” The right choice depends on the research question, ethics, available treatment and whether the study can reliably detect a real difference.
