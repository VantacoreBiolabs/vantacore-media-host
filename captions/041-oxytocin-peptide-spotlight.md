OXYTOCIN: MORE THAN THE “LOVE HORMONE” 🧬

Oxytocin is a real peptide hormone—not just a pop-science nickname.

Four quick facts:

• Oxytocin is a nonapeptide, meaning it contains nine amino acids.  
• It is made by neurons in the hypothalamus.  
• Some oxytocin travels to the posterior pituitary, where it can be released into the bloodstream.  
• Its well-established physiological roles include uterine contractions and milk ejection.

Evidence check: researchers also study oxytocin in brain signaling and social behavior, but “the love hormone” is an oversimplification. Effects can vary with context, study design, population and the outcome being measured.
