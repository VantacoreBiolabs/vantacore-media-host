PRIMARY vs SECONDARY ENDPOINTS: WHAT WAS THE TRIAL BUILT TO ANSWER? 🔬

An endpoint is a result that researchers plan to measure in a clinical trial.

Four quick facts:
• The primary endpoint addresses the trial’s main research question.
• It is specified before results are known, and the study’s design is usually built around evaluating it.
• Secondary endpoints examine additional questions and can support or expand the overall evidence.
• Testing many endpoints increases the chance of a misleading positive result unless the analysis plan handles that multiplicity.

Evidence check: a positive secondary endpoint may still be informative, but it should be interpreted alongside the primary result, the prespecified testing order and the statistical plan. It does not automatically replace a missed primary endpoint.
