PREPRINT vs PEER-REVIEWED: WHAT CHANGED? 📄

Both can share real research—but they represent different stages of scientific communication.

Four quick facts:
• A preprint is a manuscript shared publicly before formal journal peer review is complete.
• Preprints make findings available quickly and invite early feedback.
• In peer review, independent experts examine the methods, analysis and conclusions before journal publication.
• Authors may revise their paper after review, so the final published version can differ from the preprint.

Evidence check: peer review adds scrutiny, but it does not guarantee that a study is correct. Whether reading a preprint or a journal article, check the methods, sample, statistics, limitations and whether other research agrees.
