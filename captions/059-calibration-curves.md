CALIBRATION CURVES: HOW A SIGNAL BECOMES A NUMBER 📈

Laboratory instruments often produce a signal—not a ready-made concentration. A calibration curve helps translate that signal into a measured amount.

Four quick facts:
• Researchers test standards containing known amounts of the analyte.
• The instrument response is recorded at each known concentration.
• Those points create a mathematical relationship between concentration and response.
• The response from an unknown sample can then be compared with that validated relationship.

Evidence check: a calibration curve is reliable only within the range and conditions for which the method was validated. Extrapolating below or above that range can produce misleading numbers, and a strong-looking curve does not replace checks for accuracy, precision, selectivity or matrix effects.
