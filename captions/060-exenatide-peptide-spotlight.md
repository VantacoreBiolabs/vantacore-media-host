EXENATIDE: THE GILA MONSTER CLUE 🦎

Exenatide is a 39-amino-acid synthetic peptide whose story began with exendin-4, a peptide identified in Gila monster saliva.

Four quick facts:
• Exenatide activates the GLP-1 receptor.
• It helps pancreatic beta cells release insulin when glucose is elevated.
• It also suppresses inappropriately elevated glucagon and slows stomach emptying.
• It received its first U.S. approval in 2005—years before today’s newer GLP-1 medicines.

Evidence check: exenatide has established clinical evidence and an FDA-approved role in improving glycemic control for adults with type 2 diabetes. That approval does not validate every broader claim made online about GLP-1 drugs, and the current label includes important limitations, warnings and adverse effects.
