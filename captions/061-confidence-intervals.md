CONFIDENCE INTERVALS: THE RANGE MATTERS 📊

A study usually gives a best estimate plus a range that shows how uncertain that estimate is.

Four quick facts:
• The point estimate is the study’s best single estimate of an effect.
• A confidence interval gives a lower and upper limit around that estimate.
• A narrower interval generally means greater precision; a wider one means more uncertainty.
• Sample size and variability are among the factors that affect the interval’s width.

Evidence check: a 95% confidence interval is not a promise that 95% of individuals will fall inside it. In repeated studies using the same method, about 95% of the calculated intervals would be expected to contain the true value. Read both ends of the range and ask whether they would lead to different practical conclusions.
