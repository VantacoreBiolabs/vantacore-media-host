REFERENCE INTERVAL ≠ DECISION LIMIT 🧪

They may appear beside the same lab result, but they answer different questions.

• A reference interval describes values seen in a chosen reference population. Many are designed to include the central 95% of that group.
• A clinical decision limit is a threshold linked to a particular risk, diagnosis, or action in an evidence-based guideline.
• The decision limit may sit inside or outside the reference interval.
• A result outside a reference interval is a flag for interpretation—not automatic proof of disease.

Context still matters: the test method, age, sex, health state, and clinical question can all affect how a result should be read.
