POSITIVE vs NEGATIVE CONTROLS: THE CHECKS BEHIND A TRUSTWORTHY TEST 🧪

Controls help researchers tell whether an experiment worked as intended—or whether something went wrong.

Four quick facts:
• A positive control is expected to produce a known signal.
• If that signal is missing, the test may have failed to detect something it should detect.
• A negative control is expected to show no target signal.
• An unexpected signal in the negative control can reveal contamination, background noise or another source of error.

Evidence check: controls do not guarantee that every result is correct. They test specific failure points, and the right controls depend on the method and research question. If required controls fail, the sample results may not be interpretable.
