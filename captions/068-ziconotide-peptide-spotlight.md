ZICONOTIDE: THE CONE-SNAIL PEPTIDE 🐚

Ziconotide is a synthetic 25-amino-acid peptide modeled after a conopeptide found in the venom of the marine cone snail *Conus magus*.

Four quick facts:
• It binds to N-type voltage-sensitive calcium channels on pain-sensing nerve pathways in the spinal cord.
• Blocking those channels reduces the release of excitatory neurotransmitters from primary nerve terminals.
• It does not bind to opioid receptors, so its mechanism is fundamentally different from an opioid’s.
• Controlled clinical studies supported approval for a narrow severe-chronic-pain setting when other treatments are unsuitable or unsuccessful.

Evidence check: approval does not make ziconotide a general-purpose pain peptide. Its prescribing information carries a boxed warning for serious neuropsychiatric reactions, and it is contraindicated in people with a preexisting history of psychosis.
