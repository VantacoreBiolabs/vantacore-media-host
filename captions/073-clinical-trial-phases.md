CLINICAL TRIAL PHASES: WHAT CHANGES? 🔬

A phase number tells you the main research question—not whether a compound is “good” or guaranteed to succeed.

• Phase 1 introduces the investigational drug into people and focuses heavily on safety, exposure, side effects and pharmacology.
• Phase 2 looks for preliminary evidence of effectiveness in people with the condition while continuing safety study.
• Phase 3 gathers larger, more confirmatory effectiveness and safety data to assess overall benefit versus risk.
• Phase 4 happens after approval and can examine longer-term safety, effectiveness and broader real-world use.

Evidence check: phases can overlap or be combined, and development is not always a straight staircase. A later phase does not erase study limitations or guarantee approval.
