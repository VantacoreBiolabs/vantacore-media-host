EFFICACY vs EFFECTIVENESS: CAN IT WORK—or DOES IT WORK IN PRACTICE? 🔬

The words sound interchangeable, but they answer different research questions.

EFFICACY asks how an intervention performs under ideal or tightly controlled study conditions. These trials often use selected participants, standardized procedures and close follow-up.

EFFECTIVENESS asks how it performs in routine practice. Real-world populations may be broader, and results can be influenced by access, adherence, other health conditions and differences in care.

Evidence check: strong efficacy does not guarantee equally strong effectiveness. The gap is useful information—it shows how study conditions and everyday conditions can change outcomes. Individual studies can also sit somewhere between the two.
