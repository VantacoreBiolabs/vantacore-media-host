SYSTEMATIC REVIEW vs META-ANALYSIS: WHAT’S THE DIFFERENCE? 🔎

These terms are often paired, but they describe different parts of evidence synthesis.

Four quick facts:

• A systematic review starts with a focused question and a preplanned method for finding, selecting and evaluating relevant studies.
• A meta-analysis is a statistical method that combines numerical results from two or more studies.
• A systematic review can exist without a meta-analysis when the studies are too different or cannot be meaningfully combined.
• A meta-analysis may improve precision, but the pooled number is only as trustworthy as the included evidence and the analytical choices.

Evidence check: combining more studies does not automatically create stronger evidence. Differences between study populations, methods, outcomes and risk of bias still matter—and sometimes the responsible choice is not to calculate a pooled result.
