BULEVIRTIDE: THE VIRAL DOORWAY BLOCKER 🧬

Bulevirtide is a synthetic 47-amino-acid lipopeptide designed to block a doorway hepatitis D virus uses to attach to liver cells.

Four quick facts:

• The doorway is a liver-cell protein called NTCP.
• Hepatitis D virus uses NTCP as a receptor for attachment.
• Bulevirtide binds NTCP and blocks that first attachment step.
• Its U.S. indication is specific to adults with chronic hepatitis D who have no cirrhosis or compensated cirrhosis.

Evidence check: U.S. approval is accelerated, based on reduced HDV RNA and normalization of the liver enzyme ALT. Improvement in disease-related clinical outcomes has not yet been established. The label also carries a boxed warning that severe hepatitis D and B flares may occur after treatment is stopped.
