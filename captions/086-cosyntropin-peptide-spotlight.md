Cosyntropin is a peptide built to mimic the active front end of ACTH—the signal that tells the adrenal cortex to respond.

• It contains the first 24 amino acids of natural 39-amino-acid ACTH.
• That short sequence keeps the hormone’s full corticosteroid-stimulating activity.
• In a stimulation test, clinicians measure how cortisol changes after the signal.
• Its labeled role is diagnostic: screening adrenal function alongside other tests.

Important limit: one response is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Medications, cortisol-binding proteins, assay methods, timing, and the wider clinical picture can affect interpretation.
