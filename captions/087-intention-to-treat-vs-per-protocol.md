INTENTION-TO-TREAT vs PER-PROTOCOL: WHO COUNTS IN THE ANALYSIS? 🔎

The same randomized trial can produce different analysis sets—and they do not answer exactly the same question.

• Intention-to-treat, or ITT, keeps participants in the groups to which they were originally randomized.
• This helps preserve the fair comparison created by randomization.
• A per-protocol analysis uses a predefined subset that met important protocol criteria.
• Removing participants after randomization can change the balance between groups and affect the result.

Evidence check: neither label makes an analysis automatically correct. Readers should check who was included, why anyone was excluded, how missing outcomes were handled, and whether ITT and per-protocol results point to similar conclusions.
