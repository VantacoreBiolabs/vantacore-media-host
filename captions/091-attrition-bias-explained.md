WHO DROPPED OUT—AND WHY? 🔎

Attrition bias can appear when missing outcomes change the fairness of a study comparison.

• Attrition means participants leave a study or cannot be followed through the planned outcome.
• Concern grows when dropout differs between groups.
• The reasons matter: missing outcomes tied to side effects, worsening symptoms or lack of response may distort the result.
• Strong reports show how many outcomes are missing, explain why and test whether conclusions change under different assumptions.

Evidence check: a high dropout rate does not automatically prove that a result is biased, and equal rates do not guarantee safety from bias. The pattern, reasons and handling of missing data all matter.
