WHERE DID THE EXPERIMENT HAPPEN? 🔬

In vitro, ex vivo and in vivo describe different research settings—not a ranking from “bad” to “good.”

• In vitro means outside the body, often using cells, dishes or test tubes.
• Ex vivo uses tissue or organs removed from a living organism and studied outside it.
• In vivo means the work happens inside a living organism.
• As biological complexity increases, researchers usually gain realism but lose some experimental control.

Evidence check: no model automatically proves what will happen in humans. Each answers a different question, and strong conclusions usually depend on results that remain consistent across complementary models.
