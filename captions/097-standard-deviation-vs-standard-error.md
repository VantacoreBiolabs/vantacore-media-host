Standard deviation and standard error answer different questions—and swapping them can change how a graph feels.

• Standard deviation (SD) describes how spread out the individual observations are around the sample mean.
• Standard error (SE) describes how precisely the sample mean estimates the population mean.
• For a simple mean, SE = SD ÷ √n.
• A larger sample usually makes SE smaller, but it does not make the individual observations less variable.

Reading tip: when you see error bars, check whether they represent SD, SE, or a confidence interval. They are not interchangeable.

Neither SD nor SE, by itself, proves causation, clinical importance, low bias, or good data quality.
