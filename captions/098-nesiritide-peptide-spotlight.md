NESIRITIDE: A HEART SIGNAL RECREATED 🫀

Nesiritide is a recombinant copy of human B-type natriuretic peptide, or BNP—a 32-amino-acid signal normally produced by the heart.

• Its amino-acid sequence matches naturally occurring human BNP.
• BNP is released by the ventricular heart muscle in response to stretch.
• It binds a cell-surface guanylate-cyclase receptor on vascular smooth-muscle and endothelial cells.
• That signal raises cGMP, a second messenger that promotes smooth-muscle relaxation and dilation of veins and arteries.

Evidence check: nesiritide is a prescription hospital medicine approved for a specific acute-heart-failure setting. It is not a general “heart-health” peptide, and clinically important low blood pressure is a major limitation.
