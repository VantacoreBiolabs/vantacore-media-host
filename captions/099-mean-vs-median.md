MEAN vs MEDIAN: ONE EXTREME VALUE, TWO DIFFERENT STORIES 📊

Both describe the center of a dataset—but they calculate it differently.

• The mean adds every value and divides by the number of observations.
• The median sorts the values and finds the middle position.
• Because the mean uses every value, one extreme observation can move it substantially.
• The median depends on order, so it is usually less affected by extreme values.

Example: for 2, 3, 3, 4 and 50, the mean is 12.4 while the median is 3.

Evidence check: neither measure is automatically “better.” The distribution’s shape, possible outliers and the research question help determine which summary is more useful. Extreme values should be investigated—not automatically deleted.
