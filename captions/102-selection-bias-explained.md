WHO GOT INTO THE STUDY—AND HOW? 🚪

Selection bias can distort a result when the people who enter a study differ systematically from the population or comparison group the study is meant to represent.

• The problem starts with who is eligible, invited, enrolled or included in the analysis.
• Volunteers may differ from people who choose not to participate.
• Cases and controls must come from populations that can support a fair comparison.
• If selection is connected to both the exposure and the outcome, the measured association can be pushed away from the truth.

Evidence check: a large sample does not automatically remove selection bias. More observations can make a biased estimate more precise without making it more accurate.
