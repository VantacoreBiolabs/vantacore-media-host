RANDOM SAMPLING ≠ RANDOM ASSIGNMENT

They sound similar, but they solve different study problems:

• Random sampling selects who enters a study from a target population.
• It can improve how representative the sample is.
• Random assignment places enrolled participants into study groups.
• It helps make group comparisons fairer by balancing baseline differences.

Key limitation: A trial can randomly assign participants even when the people who enrolled were not randomly sampled. Assignment strengthens causal comparisons; sampling affects how broadly results may generalize.
