RELIABILITY: SAME RESULT?

There is more than one way to ask whether a measurement is consistent.

Inter-rater reliability asks whether different raters score the same thing similarly.

Test–retest reliability asks whether the same measure produces stable results when repeated over time—assuming what is measured has not truly changed.

Quick example:
• Two reviewers rate the same image = inter-rater
• One person repeats the same test later = test–retest

Evidence check: reliable does not automatically mean valid. A measure can be consistent and still measure the wrong thing.
