MORE TESTS. MORE FALSE ALARMS?

Running many statistical tests changes how a “significant” result should be read.

• Every test has some chance of producing a false positive.
• More outcomes, subgroups, or time points mean more tests.
• With ten independent null tests at a 5% threshold, the chance of at least one false positive is about 40%.
• Multiple-testing adjustments help control the overall error rate.

Evidence limit: adjustment can reduce statistical power, and the right approach depends on which tests belong to the same research question.
