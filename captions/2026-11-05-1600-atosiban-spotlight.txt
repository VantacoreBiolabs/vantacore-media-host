ATOSIBAN: THE OXYTOCIN-SIGNAL BLOCKER

This modified peptide competes with a natural hormone signal at its receptor.

• Atosiban is a synthetic nine-residue peptide related to oxytocin.
• A disulfide bridge creates its cyclic structure.
• It acts as a competitive antagonist at human oxytocin receptors.
• It also binds vasopressin receptors, so it is not completely receptor-selective.

Evidence limit: regulatory approval varies by region, and receptor binding alone does not predict every biological or clinical outcome.
