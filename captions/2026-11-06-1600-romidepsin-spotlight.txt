ROMIDEPSIN: THE DISULFIDE SWITCH

Romidepsin is a bicyclic depsipeptide originally isolated from the bacterium Chromobacterium violaceum.

Its closed structure contains a disulfide bond—a link between two sulfur atoms.

Inside cells, reduction of that bond exposes an active thiol group.

The reduced form inhibits histone deacetylase, or HDAC, enzymes involved in removing acetyl groups from proteins.

Evidence limit: This molecular switch explains part of the mechanism, but it does not predict clinical benefit, safety, or every downstream effect.
