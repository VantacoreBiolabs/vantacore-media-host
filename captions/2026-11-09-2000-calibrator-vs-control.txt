CALIBRATOR VS CONTROL: THEY DO DIFFERENT JOBS.

• A calibrator has a known or assigned value used to set the relationship between instrument signal and measurement.

• A control has an expected result or range and is tested like a sample.

• Controls help reveal test-system errors, shifts, or performance problems.

• Calibration sets the scale; control results check whether the testing run stays within its rules.

Evidence/limitation: Exact materials, frequencies, and acceptance limits depend on the method. A material generally should not serve as both calibrator and control in the same measurement procedure.
