C-TERMINAL AMIDATION: A SMALL END CHANGE WITH A BIG POSSIBLE EFFECT.

• Amidation converts a peptide’s C-terminal carboxyl group into an amide.

• This often removes the negative charge normally carried at that end.

• In cells, many amidated peptides are made from glycine-extended precursors by the PAM enzyme.

• The modification can influence peptide stability and receptor interaction.

Evidence/limitation: Amidation matters for many natural peptides, but its effect is peptide-specific. It does not automatically improve every peptide or establish benefit or safety.
