PEPTIDE CHARGE: WHY pH CAN FLIP THE SIGN.

• A peptide can carry positive and negative charges at the same time.
• Its N-terminus, C-terminus, and certain side chains can gain or lose protons.
• Lower pH usually shifts net charge in the positive direction; higher pH usually shifts it in the negative direction.
• The isoelectric point, or pI, is the pH where the peptide’s average net charge is zero.

Evidence check: charge calculations are estimates. Results depend on the sequence, local pKa values, terminal or side-chain modifications, and the solution environment.
