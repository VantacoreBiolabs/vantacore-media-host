L vs D AMINO ACIDS: SAME ATOMS. MIRROR-IMAGE ARRANGEMENT.

• Most amino acids can exist as L and D forms: non-superimposable mirror images with the same atom connections but different 3D arrangements.
• Glycine is the exception because its central carbon does not have four different groups, so it is not chiral.
• Ribosomes build proteins overwhelmingly from L-amino acids.
• D-amino acids still occur naturally, including D-alanine and D-glutamate in bacterial cell-wall material.

Evidence check: changing an L residue to a D residue can reduce recognition by some proteases, but it can also change peptide shape and target binding. The result depends on the residue, position, sequence, and assay—not the letter alone.
