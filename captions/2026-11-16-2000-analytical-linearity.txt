ANALYTICAL LINEARITY: DOES DOUBLE THE AMOUNT MEAN DOUBLE THE SIGNAL?

• Linearity asks whether an analytical procedure's response changes predictably with known concentration or amount across a stated range.
• It is assessed using several levels distributed across that range—not a single comparison point.
• A fitted line summarizes the trend; residuals show how far each observed point sits from the model's prediction.
• The useful question is whether the model performs acceptably across the intended range, not whether the graph merely looks straight.

Evidence check: a high correlation coefficient or R² alone does not establish linearity. Slope, intercept, residual patterns, range, and predefined performance criteria all matter. Some procedures are legitimately nonlinear and require a justified model instead.
