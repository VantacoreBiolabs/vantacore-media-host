APROTININ: 58 RESIDUES. THREE DISULFIDE BRIDGES. A MULTI-PROTEASE INHIBITOR.

• Aprotinin—also called bovine pancreatic trypsin inhibitor—is a single-chain peptide with 58 amino-acid residues.
• Three disulfide bridges stabilize its compact fold.
• It is the classic member of the Kunitz protease-inhibitor family.
• A binding loop occupies enzyme active sites, allowing aprotinin to reversibly inhibit several serine proteases, including trypsin, plasmin, and kallikrein.

Evidence check: “protease inhibitor” is not one-target shorthand. Aprotinin binds different proteases with different strengths, so activity in one assay does not automatically predict equal inhibition of another enzyme.
