SAME ENZYME. DIFFERENT ROADBLOCK.

Competitive and noncompetitive inhibition both slow an enzyme—but they do it differently:

• Competitive: substrate and inhibitor cannot bind at the same time.
• In the ideal competitive model, enough substrate can outcompete the inhibitor.
• Pure noncompetitive: the inhibitor binds away from the substrate's active site.
• Pure noncompetitive inhibition lowers Vmax while Km stays the same.

Evidence check: pure noncompetitive inhibition is a special case of mixed inhibition. Many real enzyme systems do not fit either ideal model perfectly.
