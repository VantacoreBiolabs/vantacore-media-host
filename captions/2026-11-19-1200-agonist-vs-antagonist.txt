AGONIST vs ANTAGONIST: SAME RECEPTOR, DIFFERENT RESULT.

Both can bind receptors—but what happens next is the key difference.

• An agonist binds and activates a receptor.
• That activation starts a signaling response.
• An antagonist binds without activating the receptor.
• By occupying or otherwise interfering with the receptor, it reduces an agonist's effect.

Important nuance: receptor pharmacology is not always binary. Partial agonists activate a receptor but produce a lower maximum response than a full agonist, while inverse agonists can shift constitutively active receptors toward an inactive state. Classification can also depend on the receptor, signaling pathway, and assay being measured.
