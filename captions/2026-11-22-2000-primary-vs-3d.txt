PRIMARY STRUCTURE vs 3D STRUCTURE: order versus spatial arrangement.

• Primary structure is the amino-acid sequence of a peptide or protein chain.
• Sequences are commonly written from the N-terminus toward the C-terminus.
• Three-dimensional structure describes how the chain and its atoms are arranged in space.
• The chain may bend, turn, and pack into different conformations.

Evidence boundary: sequence constrains folding, but it does not by itself prove that a peptide has one rigid shape. Experimental conditions and the measurement method can matter when describing structure.
