LINEAR PEPTIDE vs CYCLIC PEPTIDE: WHAT CHANGES?

• A linear peptide has an open main chain.
• A cyclic peptide contains a covalent connection that creates a ring.
• Ring closure can use peptide bonds, disulfide bonds, ester bonds, or other linkages.
• Cyclic peptides are often more conformationally restricted than comparable linear peptides.

Evidence boundary: cyclization can change shape and may improve resistance to proteolysis in some designs, but it does not automatically make a peptide stronger, more stable, more effective, or better. Sequence, ring size, linkage, environment, and the property being measured all matter.
