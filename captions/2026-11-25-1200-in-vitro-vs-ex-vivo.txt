IN VITRO VS EX VIVO: WHAT’S THE DIFFERENCE?

• In vitro means work performed outside the body in a controlled laboratory system.
• Cultured cells or cellular components in a dish or assay plate are common in vitro examples.
• Ex vivo research uses cells, tissues, or organs removed from an organism and studied while still viable.
• Ex vivo models can preserve more native tissue structure and cell-to-cell interactions than simpler cultured systems.

Evidence boundary: Neither model alone proves what will happen in a whole living body. The sample source, handling, experimental conditions, endpoints, and independent replication all affect what a result can support. Authors do not always use these labels consistently, so check the actual methods.
