CAN A STUDY SEE A REAL EFFECT? 🔎

Statistical power is the chance that a study’s test will detect a specified effect when that effect is actually present.

• Low power can miss a real signal—a false negative, also called a Type II error.
• Larger samples generally improve power.
• Larger effects and lower measurement variability are easier to detect.
• The statistical test, significance threshold and study design also matter.

Evidence check: high power does not prove an effect is important, remove bias or guarantee a good study. A large study can detect a tiny effect that has little practical meaning, so effect size, uncertainty and study quality still matter.
