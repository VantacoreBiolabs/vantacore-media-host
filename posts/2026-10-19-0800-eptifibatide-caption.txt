EPTIFIBATIDE: BLOCKING THE PLATELET BRIDGE 🩸

Eptifibatide is a synthetic cyclic heptapeptide—a compact, ring-shaped peptide used as a platelet-aggregation inhibitor.

• Its target is GP IIb/IIIa, a receptor on the platelet surface.
• Fibrinogen and von Willebrand factor normally bind this receptor and help connect platelets.
• Eptifibatide occupies the receptor and prevents those adhesive bridges from forming.
• The receptor binding is reversible, so platelet inhibition decreases after the compound dissociates.

Evidence check: eptifibatide is a prescription hospital medicine used in specific acute-coronary-syndrome and cardiac-procedure settings. It can cause serious bleeding and thrombocytopenia, so it is not a general cardiovascular or “blood-thinning” peptide.
