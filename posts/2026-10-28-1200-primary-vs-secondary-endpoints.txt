PRIMARY ≠ SECONDARY

An endpoint is a planned measurement used to evaluate what happened in a clinical trial.

The primary endpoint answers the trial’s main question and usually drives the sample-size plan.

Secondary endpoints test additional questions that were specified before the results were known.

They can support the primary result or show other meaningful effects—but they do not carry the same decision-making weight.

Evidence check: a positive secondary finding cannot automatically rescue a failed primary endpoint. Multiple endpoints also require careful statistical planning to limit false-positive conclusions.
