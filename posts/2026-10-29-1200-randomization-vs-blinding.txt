RANDOMIZED ≠ BLINDED

• Randomization assigns participants to study groups by chance rather than choice.
• Its goal is to create comparable groups and reduce bias during assignment.
• Blinding hides the assigned group from participants, researchers or outcome assessors.
• Its goal is to reduce the ways that knowing an assignment could influence behavior, care or measurement.

Evidence check: A trial can be randomized without being blinded—or blinded without true random assignment. Allocation concealment is another distinct safeguard: it prevents recruiters from knowing the next assignment before enrollment.
