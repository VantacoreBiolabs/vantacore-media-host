Measurement bias: when the ruler changes the result.

• Measurement bias is a systematic problem—not ordinary random noise.
• Groups can look different when outcomes are measured differently.
• Subjective outcomes are especially vulnerable when assessors know the assigned group.
• Using the same validated tool, timing and rules across groups helps reduce the risk.

Evidence check: standardized methods and blinded outcome assessors can lower measurement bias, but no design choice makes a study completely bias-proof.
