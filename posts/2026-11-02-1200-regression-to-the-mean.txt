REGRESSION TO THE MEAN: EXTREME TODAY, CLOSER TOMORROW?

• An unusually high or low result often looks less extreme when measured again.
• Random biological or measurement variation can help create that first outlier.
• If people enter a study because of an extreme baseline value, natural movement toward average can look like improvement.
• Repeated measurements and a concurrent control group help separate this pattern from a real intervention effect.

Evidence check: regression to the mean is a statistical pattern, not proof that no real effect exists. It is an alternative explanation researchers must consider—especially in uncontrolled before-and-after studies.
