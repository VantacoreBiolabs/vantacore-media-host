PLACEBO RESPONSE ≠ PLACEBO EFFECT

These two terms sound interchangeable, but they answer different questions.

• Placebo response means all observed change in a placebo group.
• That change can include natural symptom shifts, regression to the mean, measurement noise, and study-context effects.
• Placebo effect is the change attributed specifically to placebo-related context—such as expectation, learning, and treatment cues.
• To isolate that effect, researchers may compare a placebo group with a no-treatment or natural-history group.

Evidence limit: improvement after receiving a placebo does not prove the placebo caused the improvement.
