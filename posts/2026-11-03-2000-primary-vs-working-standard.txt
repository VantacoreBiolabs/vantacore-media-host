PRIMARY STANDARD ≠ WORKING STANDARD

Lab results need a trustworthy point of comparison—but not every standard plays the same role.

• A primary standard is extensively characterized authentic material.
• It acts as the high-confidence anchor for comparison.
• A working—or secondary—standard is qualified by comparison with the primary.
• Working standards make routine laboratory testing more practical.

Quality limit: “in-house” or “high purity” does not automatically make material a valid working standard. Its identity, quality, assigned value, stability, documentation, and traceability must fit the intended test.
